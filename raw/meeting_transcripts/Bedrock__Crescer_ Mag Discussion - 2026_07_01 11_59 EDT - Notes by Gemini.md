### Details

-   **Recent Sprint and Technology Review**: Anna Atencio and Francisco
    > Bolivar discussed their recent trip to the UK for a sprint, noting
    > the high intensity of the work
    > ([[00:00:38]{.underline}](#section)). They shared their experience
    > observing a demonstration of contact detection technology used
    > with sonar systems, noting that while the technology worked well
    > for the specific systems employed by the demonstrators, it
    > required further testing for their own applications
    > ([[00:01:52]{.underline}](#section-1)).

-   **Magnetometer Fundamentals**: Francisco Bolivar provided an
    > overview of magnetometer principles, explaining how Earth's
    > magnetic field interacts with ferrous objects on the seafloor
    > ([[00:03:37]{.underline}](#section-3)). They explained that when
    > an Autonomous Underwater Vehicle (AUV) flies near a target, the
    > ferrous object's local magnetic field creates constructive or
    > destructive interference with the Earth's total magnetic field
    > ([[00:06:18]{.underline}](#section-5)). The primary processing
    > task is to isolate the residual signal, which represents the
    > target, from the background geomagnetic and geological noise
    > ([[00:07:42]{.underline}](#section-6)).

-   **Gradiometers and Specialized Surveys**: Francisco Bolivar defined
    > a gradiometer as an instrument using multiple magnetometers to
    > provide horizontal and vertical data, which is essential for
    > detecting specific targets like unexploded ordnance. They noted
    > that while multi-magnetometer setups are standard for certain
    > regulations, it is possible to achieve similar results with a
    > single magnetometer by flying multiple passes over a target
    > ([[00:08:48]{.underline}](#section-7)).

-   **Bedrock Magnetometer Calibration**: Francisco Bolivar explained
    > that Bedrock utilizes a single, self-compensated magnetometer. To
    > ensure accuracy, the team performs calibration
    > maneuvers---typically square patterns flown at different altitudes
    > and directions---to allow the system to learn and compensate for
    > magnetic fields generated by the AUV\'s thrusters and hardware
    > ([[00:11:06]{.underline}](#section-9)). These maneuvers must be
    > conducted in areas without existing anomalies to isolate the
    > vehicle\'s own effects ([[00:13:10]{.underline}](#section-11)).

-   **Data Processing Workflow**: Geoff Horowitz and Hemanth Sarabu
    > discussed whether they would be working with raw or semi-processed
    > data ([[00:14:19]{.underline}](#section-12)). Francisco Bolivar
    > clarified that the current system includes an automated
    > cloud-based pipeline that performs \"despiking\" to remove
    > electrical interference, though the team still has access to raw,
    > uncompensated data if needed
    > ([[00:15:15]{.underline}](#section-13)). The team discussed the
    > importance of consistent filtering, noting that the pipeline
    > currently uses static, tested parameters, though manual
    > adjustments are possible for challenging datasets
    > ([[00:17:19]{.underline}](#section-15))
    > ([[00:19:09]{.underline}](#section-17)).

-   **Data Structure and Variables**: Francisco Bolivar reviewed the
    > American Standard Code for Information Interchange (ASCII) file
    > structure provided by the cloud system, which includes temporal
    > data, latitude, longitude, depth, altitude, raw vector components
    > (amplitude, X, Y, Z), and motion data like pitch, roll, and
    > heading ([[00:19:59]{.underline}](#section-18))
    > ([[00:21:59]{.underline}](#section-20)). Hemanth Sarabu clarified
    > the distinction between the \"amplitude filtered\" and \"raw\"
    > records, noting that the former includes compensation and
    > despiking ([[00:23:11]{.underline}](#section-21)).

-   **Anomalies and Pipeline Processing**: Francisco Bolivar
    > demonstrated how to visualize magnetometer data using a pipeline
    > case study. They showed how a pipeline creates distinct magnetic
    > signatures and explained the importance of cross-sectioning these
    > features ([[00:24:50]{.underline}](#section-23)). The processing
    > goal is to create a smooth ambient field baseline---often using
    > low-pass filtering or rolling averages---and subtract it from the
    > total field to leave only the residual signal, which highlights
    > local anomalies ([[00:28:41]{.underline}](#section-26)).

-   **Anomaly Picking and Analytical Signals**: The team discussed
    > methods for picking anomalies, noting that while simple residual
    > plots work for monopoles, dipolar signatures can be complex
    > ([[00:30:35]{.underline}](#section-28)). Francisco Bolivar
    > introduced the \"analytical signal\" as a tool that performs a 3D
    > derivative of the anomaly, effectively collapsing complex dipoles
    > into a single high-value peak, which simplifies identification
    > ([[00:31:37]{.underline}](#section-29)).

-   **Filtering Consistency and Thresholds**: Hemanth Sarabu and
    > Francisco Bolivar emphasized the necessity of applying consistent
    > filtering parameters across all survey lines to avoid biasing the
    > training data ([[00:19:09]{.underline}](#section-17))
    > ([[00:35:59]{.underline}](#section-33)). Regarding thresholds for
    > identifying anomalies, Francisco Bolivar stated that signals above
    > 5 nanoteslas typically require attention, while those above 10
    > nanoteslas are considered automatic anomalies
    > ([[00:38:00]{.underline}](#section-35)).

-   **Data Quality and Artifacts**: Anna Atencio highlighted that
    > variations in AUV depth or altitude can cause artificial
    > anomalies, advising caution when interpreting spikes
    > ([[00:39:14]{.underline}](#section-36)). Francisco Bolivar
    > mentioned \"upward continuation\" as a technique to handle these
    > altitude-related issues by interpolating the magnetic field to a
    > consistent level ([[00:40:15]{.underline}](#section-37)).

-   **Survey Altitude Standards**: Francisco Bolivar noted that standard
    > survey altitudes are typically 3 meters for unexploded ordnance
    > and 5 to 7 meters for pipelines, depending on the sensor and
    > objective ([[00:41:22]{.underline}](#section-38)). They explained
    > that the size of an anomaly depends on both the distance from the
    > target and the mass or susceptibility of the ferrous material
    > ([[00:42:18]{.underline}](#section-39)).

-   **Correlation with Other Sensors**: Francisco Bolivar explained that
    > after picking magnetic anomalies, the team correlates these
    > findings with data from bathymetry, side-scan sonar, and
    > sub-bottom profilers. This multi-sensor approach is crucial for
    > distinguishing between pipelines, boulders, and other seafloor
    > features ([[00:46:50]{.underline}](#section-43)).

-   **Grid Interpolation and Future Data Needs**: The team discussed the
    > use of grid interpolation to create visualizations, with Francisco
    > Bolivar noting that they typically use 5-meter spacing for these
    > surfaces ([[00:50:46]{.underline}](#section-46)). Geoff Horowitz
    > confirmed that they are working with datasets from the Danish
    > Royal Navy and other sources
    > ([[00:53:32]{.underline}](#section-48)).

-   **Model Training and Reporting**: Geoff Horowitz expressed interest
    > in how physics modeling is conducted for the magnetometer data to
    > aid in synthetic data generation
    > ([[00:54:48]{.underline}](#section-49)). Francisco Bolivar agreed
    > that future discussions would cover this, as target
    > characterization via forward modeling is the next logical step.
